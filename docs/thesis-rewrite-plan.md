# The Great Transition — proposed thesis rewrite

Approved plan · 10 September 2026. The sequence has now been completed through a first discussion draft: [The Great Transition](thesis-draft.md). Scope decisions, the causal outline and source checks are recorded in the [companion evidence note](research/thesis-evidence-2026-09-10.md). The existing `thesis.md` remains unchanged pending discussion. Confirmed audience: financially literate readers encountering the framework for the first time.

## Editorial premise

Approved framing:

> Technology accelerates a monetary transition already shaped by debt, fiscal commitments and reserve diversification.

The main argument should follow the dashboard: technological deflation → fiscal dominance → hard-money monetization. Debt, demographics and geopolitical reserve incentives remain independent drivers. Technology strengthens and potentially compresses the transition; the monetary case does not depend on a particular AGI definition or arrival date.

Reason primarily at the level of governments, currency areas, reserve managers and aggregate savings. Discuss incentives and strategic responses rather than moral judgments or household portfolio prescriptions.

## Proposed contents

### Opening: the transition already underway

A concise statement of the thesis and the three mechanisms. Introduce the distinction between expanding dollar transaction use and shifting reserve/store-of-value preferences. Establish the structural horizon, while allowing the pace of change to accelerate.

Working question: how does a system built around nominal obligations respond when productive capability becomes much cheaper and more reproducible?

### 1. Technological deflation: from capability to economic output

- Useful machine cognition: capability, cost, autonomy, parallel deployment and reliability. Use a few verified examples rather than a catalogue of model launches.
- Effective compute and AI-assisted research: explain the feedback loops, with overlapping contributors kept separate from independent multipliers.
- The deployment bridge: competent agents → reliable workflows → adoption → changes in labour inputs, unit costs and output. Benchmarks establish capability; they do not directly measure realised macroeconomic productivity.
- Robotics: the extension into physical work, including fleet updates and local/remote cognition. Cover the economic mechanism briefly; keep architecture and individual-company detail in supporting material.
- Price transmission: distinguish lower production costs, competitive pass-through, demand expansion, margins and nominal income. Identify the conditions under which productivity gains intensify fiscal pressure.
- Infrastructure constraints and uneven adoption affect the pace and distribution of deflation. Present the user's 2026–2027 acceleration view as a dated working expectation, supported by milestones that can be revisited.

Dashboard evidence: work-task benchmarks, ECI frontier gains and EBR experiments. Acknowledge that realised productivity and price pass-through require evidence beyond this dashboard's current series.

### 2. Fiscal dominance: growth, obligations and dollar demand

- Existing debt and fiscal commitments; demographics, refinancing and the demand for long-duration sovereign claims.
- The growth strategy: productivity investment, infrastructure and reshoring alongside pressure to sustain nominal incomes and service debt.
- The load-bearing accounting question: whether real-output growth and fiscal receipts outrun disinflation, financing costs and spending commitments. Explain nominal GDP rather than assuming lower unit costs automatically reduce aggregate revenues.
- Fiscal and monetary transmission: distinguish deficits, debt issuance, bank credit, central-bank accommodation and broad-money growth. Avoid treating them as interchangeable forms of “printing.”
- Dollar distribution: stablecoins, internet platforms and programmable settlement can extend access to dollars. Trace purchaser → issuer → reserve assets, and distinguish new demand from substitution out of existing dollar deposits or money-market funds.
- The maturity distinction: a stronger bid for bills does not imply an equally strong bid for 30-year debt. Explain how transaction demand can ease financing constraints while reserve preferences change.
- Smaller-currency substitution into dollars belongs here, alongside the possibility of continued dollar strength during the transition.

Dashboard evidence: debt/GDP, M2, regional money, 2Y/30Y yields and USD stablecoin supply. These are observations of the monetary environment, not proof that fiscal policy caused every change in M2.

### 3. Hard-money monetization: transaction money and reserve wealth

- Define monetization as an increasing willingness to hold an asset for monetary purposes, beyond its non-monetary uses. Separate it from a temporary price rally.
- Present the layered system: dollars for transactions, pricing and credit; Treasuries for financing and collateral; gold and Bitcoin for some portion of non-sovereign reserve and savings demand.
- Explain the thesis that manufactured goods can become cheaper in fiat terms while scarce assets gain monetary value. Falling consumer prices and declining fiat reserve appeal are distinct propositions.
- Gold and Bitcoin: compare monetary functions, issuer exposure, maturity, custody, settlement, portability, liquidity and adoption. Distinguish gold's established official role from Bitcoin's developing reserve/collateral role.
- Sovereign and collective incentives: reserve diversification, sanctions exposure and strategic accumulation. US hard-asset acquisition is a possible strategic response, not a necessary premise.
- Bitcoin's monetization/liquidity/volatility feedback as a hypothesis to evaluate, without asserting a guaranteed monotonic decline in volatility.
- Broader scarcity: one short passage on unique objects, location, human services and provenance. Explain why scarcity alone does not guarantee suitability for large-scale monetary storage.

Dashboard evidence: prices, central-bank purchases and ETF flows. Treat sentiment as context. Distinguish reserve quantities from reserve shares inflated by price appreciation.

### 4. How the transition could unfold

Describe a small number of paths through the same mechanisms: rapid deployment and accommodation; slower diffusion with continued fiscal pressure; a productivity-led improvement in fiscal sustainability. Include the possibility of near-term infrastructure inflation alongside falling costs elsewhere.

Use event-driven milestones rather than inherited A–D probabilities, price bands or an assumed fixed M2-to-Bitcoin lag. Keep the short-horizon liquidity-monitor framework in its own document.

### 5. What would change the thesis

Distinguish three levels of reassessment:

1. **Timing:** capable systems diffuse more slowly, or infrastructure and integration constrain deployment.
2. **Transmission:** productivity and receipts improve fiscal sustainability sufficiently to reduce the expected need for monetary accommodation; reserve behaviour does not evolve as expected.
3. **Asset-specific outcomes:** the monetary transition occurs but Bitcoin or gold captures less of it than expected, because of competing claims, adoption, custody, governance or technical risks.

Use persistent evidence tied to mechanisms. A particular drawdown or a single weak quarter does not by itself establish that a structural mechanism has failed.

| Treat as optional scenarios | Specific AI arrival dates; government hard-asset purchases; an illustrative gold/Bitcoin split of the aggregate monetary pool |
| Retire from the main narrative | July 2026 tape snapshot; $4,000 gold/$53,000 Bitcoin floors; A–D probabilities; fixed 18-month transmission clock; hyperscaler cash tables; obsolete UI directions |

Artificial Analysis material should not be carried into the rewrite. Use the original-publisher and Epoch sources already selected for the dashboard, subject to their respective publication terms.

## Evidence and writing rules

- Build the argument before inserting statistics. Each central link should identify its premise, mechanism, supporting evidence and a meaningful alternative outcome.
- Keep published observations, derived calculations and forward-looking judgments distinguishable in the prose.
- Re-verify dated factual claims during drafting. The worldview note is a map of the user's views, not an independently verified source for every statistic.
- Use a compact dated evidence appendix for current levels and benchmark versions; avoid repeating the dashboard's changing readings throughout the durable thesis.
- Distinguish definitions: federal versus general-government debt; observation dates versus release dates; frozen versus confiscated assets; transaction balances versus reserve allocation; cost deflation versus nominal GDP.
- Explain the useful contributions of Booth, Kurzweil, Saylor and the Dollar Milkshake framework briefly, with original reasoning doing the connective work.
- Target approximately 2,500–3,500 words for the main essay, with optional technical depth in appendices. Assume financial literacy; explain AI capability, deployment and recursive research without requiring specialist vocabulary. This length is a proposal, not an agreed constraint.
