# Notes on Global M2

July 2026

### 1. What the Composite Measures

The dashboard's Global M2 figure is a **five-bloc composite**: US + China + Eurozone + Japan + UK money supply, each converted to USD at spot. As of mid-2026 it stands at **~$105.5T**.

You will see other "global M2" figures in circulation — commonly ~$135T. Those are broader baskets (top-20 economies, GDP-weighted variants). Neither is wrong; they are different measures. The five-bloc composite is used here because it covers ~80% of globally *active* liquidity with five reliable statistical sources, and because its history is internally consistent. When comparing against external charts (e.g. BTC-vs-M2 overlays), always check which basket the chart uses.

### 3. FX Effects vs Money Creation

The USD composite conflates two things: actual money creation and currency translation. Both matter — but they mean different things for the thesis.

Current live example: the yuan has appreciated from ~7.25 to ~6.78 over the past year. That alone adds roughly **$3T** to China's USD-converted M2 — about half of the composite's headline increase — without a single new yuan being created. Conversely, yen depreciation shrinks Japan's USD contribution while the BOJ's aggregates grow.

Rule for reading the dashboard: the headline USD composite measures the **dollar-denominated size of the global money pool** (what matters for assets priced in dollars); the FX-constant growth rate measures **actual monetary expansion** (what tests the thesis's printing prediction). The dashboard shows both. A composite rising purely on FX is a weaker thesis confirmation than one rising on local-currency aggregates — flag which is driving.

### 4. Not All M2 Is Globally Active

Capital-account openness, reserve status, and the eurodollar system determine how much of each bloc's money supply actually circulates in global markets:

| Bloc | Nominal M2 | Est. Globally Active | Leakage | Key Mechanism |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| US | $23.1T | ~$19–20T | ~85% | Reserve currency. Settles ~60% of trade, ~88% of FX. $9T+ USD credit to non-banks outside the US (BIS, Q2 2025 vintage). |
| Eurozone | $17.9T | ~$8T | ~45% | Second reserve currency (~20% of reserves). Open capital account. |
| China | $52.2T | ~$4–5T | ~8% | Largest nominal M2, mostly captive. Closed capital account, $50k individual FX quota. Leakage via trade settlement, HK conduit, PBoC gold. |
| Japan | $8.0T | ~$2.5–3T | ~35% | Carry-trade channel; open capital account, domestically trapped psychology. |
| UK | $4.4T | ~$2.5–3T | ~65% | London FX hub; punches far above weight. |
| **Total** | **~$105.5T** | **~$37–38T** | **~36%** | |

Leakage estimates carry a BIS Q2-2025 vintage; re-verify annually. Two standing implications:

- **US M2 is disproportionately powerful.** Roughly half of globally active liquidity from ~22% of nominal M2. A $1T US move outweighs a $5T Chinese move. This is why US M2 acceleration (+5.6% and rising, under a hawkish Fed) is the single most thesis-relevant data point of mid-2026.
- **De-dollarisation unlocks captive pools.** If China's leakage rises from ~8% to even 15–20% through gradual capital-account opening, that adds $4–6T of globally active liquidity with zero new printing — a structural expansion of the investable pool that flows disproportionately into hard assets.

A newer sixth channel: **stablecoins**. T-bill-backed stablecoins (GENIUS Act framework) convert offshore demand for dollars into synthetic Treasury demand. This is a *re*-dollarising force on the rails layer even as reserves diversify — and it grows the dollar-denominated liquidity pool accessible to hard assets 24/7. Treat stablecoin aggregate market cap (~$300B, mid-2026) as a small but structurally important sixth term.

### 5. Transmission to BTC — The 2026 Revision

The historical relationship — BTC tracks global M2 with a ~10–12-week lag, ~83% rolling-12-month directional agreement — **broke around mid-2025** and stayed broken through H1 2026: global liquidity +12% YoY at record highs while BTC fell ~22% YoY.

The mechanism: since the January 2024 spot-ETF launch, **ETF flows are the dominant short-run transmission channel**. When they run positive, they amplify the liquidity signal; when they reverse (Nov 2025 – Jun 2026), they overwhelm it for months. Raw M2 remains the right *destination* variable on a multi-year horizon, but the *path* runs through institutional flow plumbing that did not exist in prior cycles.

Practical consequences on the dashboard:

- The **divergence clock** (months since directional agreement broke) is a first-class indicator with an 18-month invalidation line — currently ~12 months.
- US net-liquidity proxies supplement the M2 composite for short-horizon reads.
- ETF cumulative net flows are the reconnection signal to watch: divergence ends when flows and liquidity point the same direction again.
